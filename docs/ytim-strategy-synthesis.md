# YTIM / Voice Match Strategy Synthesis

_Last updated: 2026-04-24_

## Strategic Read

YTIM is not just a name-recall app. The strongest business is a high-value event networking intelligence platform.

The mobile app is the attendee wedge. The business model is strongest when sold to event organizers and sponsors who need better attendee engagement, lead quality, sponsor ROI, and post-event insight.

Voice Match can serve as the product's identity layer: a consent-based way to help users recognize people and recall context. YTIM can serve as the broader commercial platform: time, space, relationship history, Trails, sponsor analytics, and event intelligence.

## What the Pasted Business Plan Adds

The uploaded business plan frames the product around three connected customer groups:

1. Attendees who want to save time, remember names, find the right people, and follow up well.
2. Organizers who want better event intelligence, paid-attendee control, sponsor value, and operational visibility.
3. Sponsors who want better lead generation, targeted engagement, and measurable ROI.

The plan also introduces several product claims and targets that now need diligence:

- 50% reduction in wasted networking time.
- 80% improvement in name recall.
- Greater than 90% name recognition accuracy.
- Greater than 75% effectiveness for connection suggestions.
- Strong user willingness to pay.
- Revenue streams from attendees, event organizers, and sponsor data packages.
- Major-event ambition including sports, conventions, conferences, and premium gatherings.

These are good investor-facing claims only after they are backed by pilot data, survey evidence, or clearly labeled assumptions.

## Refined Product Thesis

YTIM turns a crowded event into a searchable, permissioned relationship map.

A user should be able to answer:

- Who is this person?
- Where did we meet?
- Why should I talk to them?
- Who should I meet next?
- Which conversations deserve follow-up?
- What did this event actually produce?

An organizer should be able to answer:

- Which sessions, booths, and spaces created the most engagement?
- Which sponsors received meaningful traffic?
- Which VIPs or priority attendees were underserved?
- Which paid experiences drove real networking value?
- What proof can we show sponsors after the event?

A sponsor should be able to answer:

- Which attendees engaged with us?
- Which leads are worth follow-up?
- Which interactions were meaningful, not just badge scans?
- Which activation created the strongest relationship signal?

## Positioning

### Best first positioning

Relationship intelligence for high-value events.

### Supporting line

Your Time Is Money: know who matters, meet faster, follow up smarter.

### Product architecture language

- Voice Match: consent-based identity and recall layer.
- Trails: opt-in event movement and interaction history.
- YTIM: full event relationship intelligence platform.

## Customer Segments

### Beachhead customers

Start with events where relationships are high-value and the buyer can make a fast decision:

- Executive retreats.
- Investor-founder events.
- Industry association conferences.
- Sponsor-heavy niche conventions.
- Private community gatherings.
- High-ticket local business events.

### Later expansion customers

- Large conventions.
- Professional sports hospitality events.
- Political fundraising events.
- Celebrity and entertainment events.
- Major sponsored public events.

## Product Roadmap Logic

Do not try to build the Olympics version first. Build the 50-person room version first.

The first product should prove that attendees will opt in, that recognition and recall are helpful, and that organizers or sponsors will pay for the event intelligence.

## Technical Direction

The current repository is a useful foundation because it already has an iOS skeleton for recording voice, placeholder MFCC processing, local Core Data persistence, and a SwiftUI/MVVM architecture.

The missing technical center is real audio feature extraction and matching. The next milestone is not a polished app. The next milestone is a believable local proof of life:

1. Enroll voice sample.
2. Extract features.
3. Store local signature.
4. Compare against enrolled signatures.
5. Display a confident or uncertain match.
6. Allow manual correction.

## Commercial Direction

The initial buyer should be the organizer or sponsor, not only the individual attendee. Individual users can validate delight. Organizers and sponsors validate revenue.

Recommended revenue streams:

- Pilot event fee.
- Per-attendee event license.
- Sponsor analytics package.
- Premium attendee concierge mode.
- Post-event CRM export package.

## Investor Story

The investor story should be reframed from a loan-style plan into a venture-style narrative:

1. Big pain: high-value networking is still inefficient.
2. Clear wedge: events where time and introductions are expensive.
3. Product magic: identity, context, suggestions, and Trails.
4. Buyer value: organizers and sponsors get measurable ROI.
5. Data moat: consented event relationship graph gets better with usage.
6. Expansion: event app to relationship intelligence platform.

## Due Diligence Gaps

These items should be resolved before serious fundraising or bank financing:

1. Confirm legal entity, founder names, ownership, and IP assignment.
2. Replace placeholder TAM with a real bottom-up model.
3. Replace placeholder survey claims with survey methodology and sample details.
4. Validate privacy requirements for voice, face, location, and event data.
5. Decide whether facial recognition remains in scope for MVP or is delayed.
6. Build pilot metrics before using accuracy and effectiveness claims externally.
7. Create realistic Year 1 cost model and infrastructure assumptions.
8. Validate willingness to pay with actual buyer interviews.
9. Create a pilot contract and data-processing addendum.
10. Define whether GitHub is source of truth and GitLab is mirror, or vice versa.
